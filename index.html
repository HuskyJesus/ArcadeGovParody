Somebody put an arcade
in the West Wing.

Five little games, published under a federal seal, teaching five little lessons: that a wall is a triumph, that people are a score, that lunch is the state's business, that a bill should sail through unopposed, and that your child's future arrives with a politician's name printed on the account.

So here are five games back. Same mechanics. Opposite premises. Play them and see which set you'd rather your kids grow up inside.

five cabinets · keyboard or touch · nothing to install

LIVING STONES

Build a house, not a barrier.

The same falling blocks. But the stones are timber and brick, and every line you clear is a household with a name, taken in and given a roof.

Answers Build the Wall

THE NINETY-NINE

Leave the ninety-nine. Go after the one.

The same growing trail behind you — except it isn't a catch. It's a flock. Every one has a name, and you walk them to a gate that stands open.

Answers Rio Run

HONEST SCALES

A just weight is the whole law of trade.

The same conveyor, the same two keys. But you aren't inspecting other people's lunch against a slogan. You're a merchant, and the only thing you reject is a lie on a label.

Answers Supply Line

ENUMERATED

Carry the bill through every gate in its way.

The same eagle, the same bill, the same one-button flight. But nothing sails through here. Committee, both chambers, the courts — and every earmark you pick up makes the thing heavier.

Answers Flappy Bill

TEN TALENTS

It is not what you catch. It is what it's for.

The same basket, the same falling money. But nobody opens an account in your child's name and stamps a politician on it. You earn it, you split it, and every subsidy you grab comes with a string that shrinks your basket.

Answers Trump Savings Tycoon
