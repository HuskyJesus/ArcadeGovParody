Somebody put an arcade
in the West Wing.

Five little games, published under a federal seal, teaching five little lessons: that a wall is a triumph, that people are a score, that lunch is the state's business, that a bill should sail through unopposed, and that your child's future arrives with a politician's name printed on the account.

So here are five games back. Same mechanics. Opposite premises. Play them and see which set you'd rather your kids grow up inside.

Build the WallTETRIS Stack the barrier fast enough and you win. The people on the other side are "the horde" — a rising quantity, never a face. Living StonesSAME FALLING BLOCKS The identical skill — fitting shapes under pressure — aimed at a roof instead of a rampart. A cleared line isn't a repelled wave. It's the Ruiz family, housed.

Supply LineCONVEYOR SORTING You're the inspector. Food is approved or rejected against a political slogan someone else wrote. Honest ScalesSAME CONVEYOR, SAME TWO KEYS You're the merchant, not the censor. The only ground for refusal is a false label — and refusing an honest supplier costs you exactly as much as selling a lie.

EnumeratedSAME ONE-BUTTON FLIGHT Every obstacle is a check the founders put there on purpose. Riders make the bill heavier; struck clauses make it fly. Friction is the feature.

Grab the subsidy and you get the cash and the string — and the string shrinks the basket for the rest of your life.
