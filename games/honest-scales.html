Answers: Supply Line (sorting)

Their cabinet puts you at a school-lunch conveyor, approving and rejecting food against a slogan — an administration's nutrition standard, rendered as a reflex test. The lesson underneath is that judging what other people may eat is a game.

Ours keeps the conveyor and the two-key sort exactly. It changes who you are and what you may refuse.

THE ARGUMENT

A free market is not a market without rules. It is a market whose one indispensable rule is that the label tells the truth. Fraud is not a market activity; it is the thing markets exist in spite of. This is why the oldest commercial law in the Hebrew Bible is not about what you may sell, but about the weights you sell it with.

And so the second half of the mechanic matters as much as the first. Refuse an honest supplier and you have not been careful; you have wronged a man who came to you in good faith and sent him home with nothing. A power to reject that answers to no standard beyond your own preference is not stewardship. It is just power.

“A false balance is an abomination to the Lord, but a just weight is his delight.” Proverbs 11:1 · see also Deuteronomy 25:13–15
